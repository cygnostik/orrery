# Construction references — supported moons and exhibition finishes

## Maker evidence, not guessed hidden machinery
Staines & Son explicitly describe separate orbital and moon gear trains on each outer-planet arm of their Genesis models.[1]
Their Tellurion description identifies a handle-driven worm and worm wheel, showing that fine display instruments route motion through real transmission elements.[2]
In their maker interview, Derek Staines describes taking the moon drive from a large ring gear and transmitting it along a shaft to the moons.[3]

These references inform an original exhibition model. They do not establish that Orrery reproduces the makers' concealed mechanisms, dimensions or gear ratios. The model's edge crank uses a 1:1 miter pair rather than the Tellurion's worm drive; see [crank-drive.md](crank-drive.md).

## Exhibit construction

The instrument combines a titanium/platinum/steel and glass body, black-DLC lower gear bank, continuous black-PVD planet and moon supports, and polished black-opal cabochon inlays. Functional perimeter fasteners remain exposed. Saturn ring bands use natural-color references described in [materials.md](materials.md).

One crank transmission controls Mechanical output motion and actual planet/moon mount positions. Smooth supports carry separate slender shafts and nested sleeves. The enclosed lunar reductions preserve integer-tooth ratios but are schematic: their internal gears are not rendered or packaged hardware. No independent output motors or additional animation clock are modeled.

Clearance tests cover named parent/moon globes, local arms, sleeves, tilted Saturn rings and neighbouring family envelopes. They do not prove free movement of every solid part or manufacturing readiness. Observatory retains its separate astronomical/schematic display.
